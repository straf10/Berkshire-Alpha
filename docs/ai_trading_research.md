Architecture and Execution Blueprint: Autonomous Multi-Agent Options Trading SystemExecutive Strategy SummaryThe deployment of an autonomous options trading agent within a condensed four-session evaluation window necessitates a fundamental departure from traditional long-horizon derivative strategies. Operating under the constraints of the Alpaca paper-trading environment, the system must execute a defined-risk, short-horizon alpha generation model. The target universe is strictly confined to highly liquid mega-cap equities—SPY, QQQ, AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMD, TSLA, META, AMZN, and GOOGL—to guarantee tight bid-ask spreads and reliable fill dynamics across complex multi-leg orders.In a competitive setting evaluated over merely four active trading sessions, conventional 45-Days-to-Expiration (DTE) option selling strategies are mathematically suboptimal. The theta decay curve at 45 DTE is too linear and flat to generate meaningful profit and loss (P&L) within the hackathon's evaluation timeframe. Consequently, the strategic focus pivots to 0–7 DTE defined-risk vertical spreads, capitalizing aggressively on the Volatility Risk Premium (VRP) and short-term directional momentum. The core alpha thesis relies on the persistent overestimation of implied volatility (IV) relative to realized volatility (RV) in the short-dated options market, a structural phenomenon where market makers demand a premium for providing tail-risk insurance.The system operates autonomously on a chronological execution loop aligned with United States market hours, translated to the local time in Thessaloniki, Greece (Eastern European Summer Time, EEST, UTC+3). United States equity markets open at 09:30 ET (16:30 EEST) and close at 16:00 ET (23:00 EEST). To circumvent the severe bid-ask spread widening and chaotic price discovery that occurs at the opening and closing bells, the multi-agent pipeline is scheduled to run entry scans twice per session: at 17:15 EEST (45 minutes post-open) and 21:00 EEST (two hours pre-close). Deterministic risk management and position monitoring loops operate at a high frequency (every five minutes) throughout the entire 16:30–23:00 EEST window.The orchestration layer adapts the TradingAgents framework, utilizing a multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) pipeline to synthesize fundamental catalysts, sentiment velocity, and quantitative technicals. To prevent the LLM pipeline from executing unbounded risk or hallucinating invalid parameters, the architecture establishes a strict separation of concerns. LLMs act purely as signal generators and debate engines, while trade sizing, Greek exposure limits, and execution mechanics are governed exclusively by hardcoded, deterministic mathematical gates.When the quantitative classifier identifies a rich IV environment (where the IV/RV ratio exceeds 1.2) accompanied by steep volatility skew, the agent constructs out-of-the-money (OTM) Credit Spreads (e.g., Bull Put Spreads or Bear Call Spreads) to harvest the volatility crush and rapid gamma-theta decay. Conversely, when IV is cheap relative to historical movement, the agent deploys in-the-money (ITM) or at-the-money (ATM) Debit Spreads, neutralizing the volatility risk while maximizing directional delta exposure to capture momentum.Mathematical Formulations and Alpha GenerationVolatility Risk Premium and Implied Volatility SkewThe foundational decision to initiate a net-credit structure versus a net-debit structure relies on quantifying the Volatility Risk Premium. The system calculates an IV/RV ratio for each underlying asset in the universe. Because Alpaca does not provide continuous historical implied volatility time series via its free data tier, the agent relies on an instantaneous cross-sectional proxy extracted from live snapshots.Realized Volatility ($RV$) is computed over a 20-day trailing window using daily logarithmic returns. The annualized realized volatility is defined as:$$RV_{20} = \sqrt{252} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (R_i - \bar{R})^2}$$Where $N=20$, $R_i = \ln(P_i / P_{i-1})$, and $\bar{R}$ is the mean of the logarithmic returns. Implied Volatility ($IV$) is extracted directly from the Alpaca options snapshot endpoint for at-the-money contracts expiring within the targeted 3–7 DTE window. The volatility richness signal is then formulated as:$$VRP_{Ratio} = \frac{IV_{ATM}}{RV_{20}}$$A $VRP_{Ratio} \ge 1.25$ indicates that options are richly priced relative to the asset's recent historical movement, triggering the agent pipeline to propose net-credit structures that sell premium.Volatility skew acts as a secondary quantitative filter, measuring the asymmetry in institutional demand for downside protection versus upside speculation. Vertical skew (or strike skew) is calculated by taking the difference in implied volatilities between the 25-delta put and the at-the-money option:$$Skew_{Abs} = IV_{25\Delta Put} - IV_{ATM}$$During periods of elevated market fear or impending catalysts, the 25-delta put commands a significant premium over ATM options, steepening the skew curve. If the absolute skew exceeds historical baselines (e.g., $> 5$ percentage points for the S&P 500), the system favors Bull Put Spreads, strategically selling the inflated 25-delta put and buying a lower-strike put to define the downside risk.Technical Indicators for Spread EntryTo augment the volatility metrics, the quantitative node of the agent pipeline calculates three primary technical indicators to confirm directional momentum and optimal entry timing over the 3–5 day horizon.1. Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) Deviation:
VWAP serves as the primary intraday trend baseline. The deviation of the current price from the VWAP indicates short-term overextension or accumulation.$$VWAP = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M} (P_{typ, j} \cdot V_j)}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} V_j}$$Where $P_{typ, j} = \frac{High_j + Low_j + Close_j}{3}$ and $V_j$ is the volume for period $j$. The VWAP deviation signal is normalized as a percentage: $Dev = \frac{P_{current} - VWAP}{VWAP} \times 100$.2. Relative Strength Index (RSI):
A short-period RSI (e.g., 5-period or 9-period) is utilized to identify immediate exhaustion in price movement, signaling mean reversion opportunities ideal for credit spread entries.$$RSI_n = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + RS} \quad \text{where} \quad RS = \frac{\text{Average Gain over } n \text{ periods}}{\text{Average Loss over } n \text{ periods}}$$3. Volume-Weighted Momentum (VWM):
Standard momentum is adjusted by volume to confirm the conviction behind a price move.$$VWM_t = (Close_t - Close_{t-n}) \cdot \ln(V_t)$$High positive VWM combined with $VRP_{Ratio} < 1.0$ provides the highest win-rate probability for entering long call debit spreads, as the directional conviction is strong and the volatility premium is cheap.Portfolio Greek Limits and Fractional Kelly SizingPosition sizing must aggressively balance geometric growth over the brief 4-day evaluation window against the risk of catastrophic account drawdown. The Kelly Criterion maximizes the long-term geometric growth rate of capital, but full Kelly sizing is notoriously volatile and can lead to unacceptable drawdowns in short-term trading. The system employs a Half-Kelly fraction adapted specifically for the binary-like, defined-risk payoffs of vertical spreads.Given a probability of success $p$ (derived from the short option's delta, e.g., a 30-delta short strike implies a rough 70% probability of expiring out-of-the-money), the expected win size $W$ (maximum profit), and the expected loss size $L$ (maximum theoretical loss), the optimal capital allocation fraction $f^*$ is:$$f^* = \left( \frac{p \cdot W - (1-p) \cdot L}{W \cdot L} \right) \times 0.5$$To strictly adhere to the deterministic Fund Manager gates, the output of the Kelly formula is capped at a hard maximum of 1.5% of the total account equity per trade.Furthermore, to prevent the multi-agent system from unilaterally stacking directional or volatility risk, the deterministic gate calculates aggregate portfolio Greeks.
The aggregate portfolio Delta ($\Delta_P$) and Vega ($\mathcal{V}_P$) are constrained relative to the total account equity ($E$):$$\Delta_P = \sum_{i=1}^{K} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{legs} \delta_{i,j} \cdot \omega_{i,j} \right) \cdot S_i \cdot 100 \le 0.15 \cdot E$$$$\mathcal{V}_P = \sum_{i=1}^{K} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{legs} \nu_{i,j} \cdot \omega_{i,j} \right) \cdot 100 \le 0.02 \cdot E$$Where $\delta_{i,j}$ and $\nu_{i,j}$ are the respective delta and vega for leg $j$ of position $i$, $\omega_{i,j}$ is the contract quantity, and $S_i$ is the underlying price.Multi-Agent Options Pipeline ArchitectureThe system modifies the TradingAgents framework to function within a multi-leg derivatives context. This ensures that all components of the spread (up to a maximum of 4 legs) are executed simultaneously, eliminating the severe leg risk associated with partial strategy execution in volatile markets.Using alpaca-py, the LimitOrderRequest must define order_class=OrderClass.MLEG and include an array of OptionLegRequest objects. Each leg requires a symbol, a ratio_qty (the proportional quantity of the leg within the spread), a side (BUY or SELL), and a position_intent.The position_intent parameter is rigorously enforced by the broker. It must explicitly state the intended action for the specific leg: BUY_TO_OPEN, SELL_TO_OPEN, BUY_TO_CLOSE, or SELL_TO_CLOSE.Furthermore, the limit price of an mleg order in the Alpaca API follows a strict directional convention. A positive limit price indicates a net debit (a cost paid by the trader), whereas a negative limit price signifies a net credit (premium received by the trader).The Limit-Order Pegging and Walking AlgorithmA critical pitfall in automated options trading is crossing the bid-ask spread with market orders, which results in devastating slippage on illiquid option contracts. Furthermore, Alpaca's paper-trading simulation engine deliberately introduces latency and random partial fills to test an algorithmic system's resilience. Paper trading fills are determined strictly by the National Best Bid and Offer (NBBO) quotes—limit orders only execute when the limit price crosses the current bid or ask; the last traded price is entirely irrelevant to the simulation engine.To guarantee fills without surrendering excessive edge to the market maker, the system utilizes a Limit-Order Walking algorithm specifically adapted for multi-leg structures:Calculate Mid-Price: Identify the current NBBO for all individual legs and calculate the theoretical mid-price of the combined spread.Initial Submission: Submit the mleg limit order exactly at the calculated mid-price.Timer Loop: Wait for $N$ seconds (e.g., 15 seconds) to allow the order to rest on the simulated order book.State Check via CLI/API: Query the order status. If the status is FILLED, terminate the loop. If the status is PARTIALLY_FILLED, log the event but do not cancel the order, as Alpaca paper trading frequently simulates partials over a multi-minute window to test asynchronous handling.Cancel and Replace (Repricing): If the order remains NEW or ACCEPTED but unfilled, execute a cancel/replace command. Adjust the limit price by a predefined tick increment ($\Delta p$, e.g., $0.05), stepping towards the "natural" price (the ask for debit spreads, the bid for credit spreads).Hard Price Cap: If the walking price hits the trader's predefined maximum acceptable slippage (e.g., 70% of the distance from the mid-price to the natural price), the order is hard-canceled and logged as UNFILLED_REJECT. The system accepts the opportunity cost of missing the trade rather than paying an extortionate bid-ask spread.Assignment, Early Exercise, and Book UnwindingTrading short-dated options inherently introduces the operational tail-risk of early assignment on short legs. If an underlying equity moves deep in-the-money (ITM) prior to expiration, the short call or put may be assigned by the clearinghouse, leaving the paper account holding a massive, unintended long or short position in the underlying equity shares.To mitigate this catastrophic failure mode, the deterministic execution loop includes a strict Assignment Reconciliation Routine. Every five minutes, the system queries the Alpaca Command Line Interface (CLI) for open positions. If any non-option symbol (an equity ticker like AAPL or SPY) is detected in the portfolio, the system assumes an assignment has occurred. It immediately generates a marketable limit order to liquidate the equity position, neutralizing the delta risk, and simultaneously closes the corresponding long option leg that was serving as protection.Because the hackathon judging concludes precisely at 15:00 UTC (11:00 ET / 18:00 EEST) on Friday, September 4, the agent is programmed with a hard end-of-competition unwind protocol. This ensures the book is entirely flat and all P&L is realized prior to the final judging snapshot, avoiding marks against stale overnight quotes.Deterministic Risk GatesRegardless of the LLM pipeline's enthusiasm or the consensus achieved during the debate phase, the Fund Manager Gate operates as a deterministic, mathematically hardcoded Python class that screens all proposed trades.
